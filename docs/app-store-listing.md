## Description — English

```
Life Abroad Simulator is a quiet text-based life sim about your first week
living alone in Tokyo. No combat, no leveling. Just messages, choices, and the
slow weight of stats you didn't notice draining.

You arrive on Day 1 with a half-empty suitcase, ¥50,000, and a number from
your mom you keep meaning to call back. Over 7 in-game days you'll work
night shifts at a convenience store, fight forms at the ward office, drink
with a coworker you barely understand, and meet a neighbor who keeps
leaving notes. Every reply changes something — but you only see how when
the week ends.

Features
• 7-day story, ~20–30 minutes per playthrough
• 5 endings — Belonging, Growth, Survive, Burnout, Go Home
• 45 events with branching responses and memory (NPCs remember what you
  said three days ago)
• Stats that breathe: money, energy, stress, language, relationship
• 3 difficulties — "easier", "balanced", "heavier" — same story, different
  starting point and recovery rate
• Atmospheric scene art for each Tokyo location
• Save persists locally. No account, no email, no cloud.

Note: this is a season 1 playtest (7 days). Week 2 is in development. If you
finish all 5 endings, the credits hint at what's next.

Best played in short sessions before bed. Headphones optional but it's
nicer with them.
```

## Category

- **Primary:** Games → Simulation
- **Secondary:** Games → Role Playing

Rationale: gameplay is choice-driven narrative simulation, not action or puzzle.
"Role Playing" pulls in audience interested in branching narrative; "Adventure"
would also work but Simulation more accurately reflects daily stat management.

## Screenshot checklist — 5 shots to capture

See `app-store-screenshots.md` for full caption text and capture
instructions. Five locations to shoot:

1. **Home screen** with the season tagline and MISSION pill
2. **Mom chat moment** on Day 1 — clear "writing to mom" feel
3. **Day Recap card** with stat deltas (Day 2 or Day 3 transition)
4. **Konbini night shift** event — atmospheric scene visible
5. **Ending screen** — pick "Belonging" or "Growth" for warmest visual

Order matters: Apple shows the first screenshot largest in search. Lead with
Home (#1) because it sets context — text-based game in Tokyo. Save Ending
(#5) for last to imply narrative payoff without spoiling.
